Rules & modesMap

Pick a place in the bar (or press 1 to 6), or click a temple, the tank or anyone, for a close-up; Whole island (or Esc) goes back. Close in on a temple, the tank or any lane and the houses around sink to stubs so you can see in. Drag to move around the island; shift-drag (or right-drag, or the turn buttons) to turn it. Everything is at one scale, 1 world unit to 4 m, except that the landmarks are drawn larger than life so they stand out from the houses (Uma Maheshwar, Bagh Bhairab and Chilancho by about a third, Lohan Dega by nearly half), and people are drawn larger so you can see them. Turn on Measure and click two points for the straight distance, the walking distance along the mapped lanes, and the climb between them. With Measure off, click the ground for its height above sea level. People walk the real lane network to real places; click anyone to see where they are going and how far they have walked.

How it's drawn

The diorama is about six places, defined once in places.js (position, ground they own, close-up framing, the radius of houses that collapse round them, what people do there) and read by everything else: Uma Maheshwar, Lyaku Museum, Bagh Bhairab, Dey Pukhu, Lohan Dega and Chilancho. Each is modelled from the photos (Commons, Mapillary, the reference set) on its OSM spot; the Places bar and the keys 1 to 6 fly to them. Lyaku is the museum in a Newar palace house with the dry-stone wall and carved gateway of its stepped lane; Lohan Dega is the stone shikhara with its stepped plinth, arcade and corner pavilions, beside the two golden Buddhas.

Built from data, not by eye. Building footprints, lanes, ponds and named squares come from an OpenStreetMap export; the ground is SRTM 30 m elevation, interpolated to 2 m cells as a smooth surface tinted in 2 m height bands. OSM rarely records heights here. Every house is drawn in the Kirtipur Hill style on the rectangle of its OSM footprint: Newar brick with carved windows on each storey under a tiled two-slope roof with struts, or painted concrete with glazed windows, a shutter, a flat roof and a water tank. Its height starts from GHSL (the EU's satellite-derived average building height, about 90 m cells), nudged down for small plots and up for large ones. That is then checked against Mapillary street photos: 450 photos along the lanes show mostly 4 to 5 storeys and rarely more than 6, so the estimate is scaled to match and capped at 6; and Mapillary's 3D reconstructions measure the facades of about 200 buildings directly, so none is drawn lower than its measured roofline. Heights are whole 3 m storeys, seated on the terrain. Bagh Bhairab and its compound come from the OSM outlines, about 90 Wikimedia Commons photos (kept in ref/bhairab with their credits) and a satellite tile: the temple faces west-north-west down a long stone path, front on its short side, back on the valley edge with its railing and big trees; a cream ground storey in a timber frame under a deep red-fringed tiled roof, a carved second storey under brown tiles, a grey metal top roof with its row of finials; the named shrines (Yamaleshwar, Narayan, Bhawani Shankar, Ram Chandra, Mana Binayek, Nasa Deu, Indrayani, Mul Ganesh) in their own styles. Their exact spots in the court are estimated. Uma Maheshwar and Chilancho get the same treatment at true scale: Uma's three-step brick plinth, mossy roofs, stone elephants, railed hilltop plaza and arched gate over the steps; Chilancho's main stupa, its four mapped corner stupas, the iron fence and gate with stone lions, brick chaityas, the red-fringed pati and prayer flags. Dey Pukhu is a sunken tank at its real 25 × 13 m: brick walls with a grey band at the water, duckweed, a fountain, a niched brick parapet, stairs down at the far corners, and the white Narayan shrine with a small pagoda and a stone pillar at its west end. Each site is levelled and paved, and nearby houses are left out. The Fewer houses slider leaves out a share of the ordinary houses (20% to start): each house has a fixed rank and they are merged in rank order, so thinning is just a shorter draw range on a few meshes, instant at any setting. Small mapped shrines elsewhere become little pagodas. People are drawn about 1.8× life size, and Bagh Bhairab's temple a little larger and taller, so they read at this scale; nothing else is exaggerated.

Roads are ribbons on their true OSM lines and widths, draped on the ground and surfaced as the Mapillary photos show: stone-flag lanes with dark gutters, stepped lanes, the asphalt main road with its dashed line and dusty shoulders, concrete or earth tracks, iron manhole covers; squares and temple courts are paved in herringbone brick or stone flags. The stretch from Bagh Bhairab's gate to Dey Pukhu is dressed from OSM points and the Mapillary street photos: every mapped pati shelter, shopfronts with goods hanging at the door at the mapped shops and tea shops, Sankhadhar Sakhwa's statue on Prajaa Dabu, poles with bundles of wires, parked motorbikes and cars, blue water drums. Close up, houses near the focus sink to stubs in the vertex shader (shadows too), so the slider and the merged meshes are untouched. Walkers route along the OSM lane graph with Dijkstra, so their distances are real. Labels are HTML pinned to world points. An orthographic camera at the true isometric angle renders into a low-res target with outlines from depth.
